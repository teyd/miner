# miner v0 — Hostile Design Critique

Reviewed: 2026-10-29. Target: `docs/history/07-proposal-v0.md`, judged against `docs/research/01`–`06`, `docs/decisions.md`, `docs/open-questions.md`, and DevilXD/TwitchDropsMiner master @ 2026-10-28 (`/tmp/research-refs/TDM`) as the behaviour reference.

## 1. Verdict

**Do not build v0 as written.** The layering (pure reducer, one `Twitch` seam, wire-level twin, recorder, replay) is the right shape and follows research 04 §6, but the design has five flaws that each force a state-machine change rather than a refactor: the event model cannot see the one signal every piece of switching logic depends on (per-channel socket topics), the reducer has no representation of progress or of a claim in flight, the claim path excludes exactly the drops that are most valuable, the twin's campaign model omits the rules that decide whether a watched minute counts at all, and the per-IP token bucket inside the seam turns "over budget" into unbounded virtual waiting under `TestClock`. Fix those five, plus the claim-window contradiction inherited from the research, before milestone 1. Everything else here is cheaper to fix later.

## 2. Critical Flaws

### CF-1: The event model is blind to a channel going live (no per-channel topics, no topic budget, one socket)

- **Where:** §1 `Event` union (`Tick, CatalogRead, InventoryRead, StreamInfo, WatchResult, ClaimResult, PubSubMessage, AuthFailed, Error`), §2 _"The PubSub socket is one fiber"_, §3 ops, and open question 6.
- **Why it sinks the design:** TDM's switching is entirely event-driven and needs **two topics per tracked channel**: `video-playback-by-id.<id>` (`viewcount`/`stream-up`/`stream-down`) and `broadcast-settings-update.<id>` (game change) — `constants.py:498-511`, consumed at `twitch.py:1129-1180`, driving `on_channel_update` (`twitch.py:1182-1237`). It tracks up to `MAX_CHANNELS = ((8×50)−2)/2 = 199` channels over a pool of up to 8 sockets with 50 topics each, and throws when the budget is exhausted (`constants.py:118-123`, `websocket.py:307-315, 371-393`). The proposal has no `ChannelLive`/`ChannelOffline`/`ChannelGameChanged` event and no `SubscribeChannels`/`UnsubscribeChannels` command, so subscriptions cannot be expressed in the reducer at all. It also drops TDM's 120 s `ONLINE_DELAY` debounce, which exists because `stream-up` arrives before stream data does (`constants.py:129`, `channel.py:386-403`).
- **Concrete scenario:** campaign for game G with a 40-channel ACL; none live at plan time. At T+7 min an ACL channel goes live. Nothing arrives on a socket, no `ReadStream` is issued for it, so the miner learns at the hourly rescan and sits idle for 53 min with a minable campaign and an eligible live channel. The twin invariant (§4: "no idle time while an eligible live channel exists") fires on the first seed; satisfying it without events requires polling every channel every few seconds, which breaks the ~5 GQL req/s per-IP budget (02 TL;DR).
- **Fix:** add the three channel events, add subscription commands derived from `State` (not free-running), model the socket as a subscription manager with an explicit topic budget in config, enforce socket and topic caps in the twin, and keep a provisional-live debounce (`stream-up` → re-read after N seconds, `channel.py:386-403`).

### CF-2: There is no progress model, and the design leaves as an open question something TDM answers with three reads

- **Where:** §1 `Watching` holds "the channel, campaign, drop and last-credit time"; §3 ops (no current-drop read); open question 3.
- **Why it sinks the design:** credit is observable three ways. (a) `user-drop-events` `drop-progress` frames carrying `drop_id` + `current_progress_min` (`twitch.py:1239-1297`). (b) `DropCurrentSessionContext` → `currentUser.dropCurrentSession.{dropID, currentMinutesWatched}` (`constants.py:353-361`, used at `twitch.py:996-1010`). (c) `Inventory` → per-drop `self.currentMinutesWatched` / `dropInstanceID` / `isClaimed` (`inventory.py:84-92, 228-237`). TDM also keeps a local optimistic estimate (`extra_current_minutes`) capped at `MAX_EXTRA_MINUTES = 15`, after which it _stops trusting itself_ and forces a channel switch (`inventory.py:293-296, 310-317`; `constants.py:117`). The proposal has none of the three reads and no estimate: its only correction is the 60 s inventory poll. That makes "Claim them as soon as they complete" (Goals) unimplementable — completion is discovered up to a poll late, and until then the plan is acting on a belief the code cannot falsify.
- **Concrete scenario:** two campaigns, same game, same channel, 90 and 240 minutes remaining. Twitch progresses one of them (research 02 §3, TDM #756). The planner orders by (game priority, end time) and believes the 240-minute one is progressing; 40 minutes later inventory shows the other one at +40 and this one at 0. Nothing in the design bounds how long a wrong belief persists or forces a re-read.
- **Fix:** add `ReadCurrentDrop`/`CurrentDropRead`; add `estimatedMinutes` + `estimatedAt` per (campaign, drop) to `State`; add a disagreement rule that adopts read truth and forces a bounded re-plan (TDM's 15-minute cap); make the twin's credit rule name _which_ drop progresses, deterministically and with a cited source (research 04 §2).

### CF-3: The claim path loses the product — ended campaigns are unplannable, the window is undefined, and "never claim twice" has no identity

- **Where:** §1 Planner ("eligible campaigns … active; not finished"), §2 _"Claims never retry"_, §4 invariant "never claim twice or early", §6 store.
- **Why it sinks the design:**
  1. TDM claims from "expired and active campaigns" (`twitch.py:730-737`) with a window of campaign end + 24 h (`inventory.py:143-151`). The proposal's planner filters on `active`, so a drop that completes at 23:55 in a campaign ending 00:00 is never claimed — the single highest-value action in the product, silently skipped.
  2. The twin's claim window is "7 days" (research 04 §2) while TDM implements 24 h (`inventory.py:143-151`) and research 02 §3 says both. A twin rule that is wrong by six days _passes_ plans that live Twitch rejects — the sim bakes the bug in.
  3. Claim identity has two shapes for the same drop: `dropInstanceID` from inventory (`inventory.py:84-92`) and `f"{user_id}#{campaign_id}#{drop_id}"` (`inventory.py:156-160`). A "claimed twice" detector keyed on the string misses the real duplicate.
  4. Success has three shapes: TDM accepts `ELIGIBLE_FOR_ALL` and `DROP_INSTANCE_ALREADY_CLAIMED` (`inventory.py:200-215`), and a GQL error means _unknown_, not failed (`inventory.py:190-197`). "Claims never retry" without a success classification cannot distinguish "already claimed" from "network ate it".
  5. Crash safety: nothing says a claim _intent_ is durable before the mutation, yet the twin's "state survives a crash" invariant plus "never auto-retry a claim" (AGENTS.md, decisions 11) require a write-ahead record — otherwise restart either loses a claim or re-sends one.
- **Concrete scenario:** the 23:55 claim above. Live: drop lost. Sim: PASS, because the twin says claims are valid for days.
- **Fix:** keep every campaign in `State`; plan _work_ from active ones and plan _claims_ from `canClaim = dropInstanceID ∧ ¬claimed ∧ now < campaignEnd + W`; pick one W, cite it, and add a lane-D experiment if the help page and TDM still disagree; key claims on `(campaignID, dropID)`; write the intent before the mutation and clear it only from an inventory read.

### CF-4: The twin's campaign model omits the rules that decide whether a minute counts at all

- **Where:** §4 "campaigns, channels …, account inventory, and credit rules from research 05"; research 04 §2's twin state list.
- **Why it sinks the design:** three real rules are missing from both the proposal and the research list, and `Watching` ("channel, campaign, drop") has no field for any of them.
  - **Precondition chains:** a drop with `preconditionDrops` earns nothing until those drops are claimed, and campaign minutes are the max over the chain (`inventory.py:97-105, 262-271`). The planner orders campaigns and picks a drop; the twin credits "the drop". Pick the wrong one and hours vanish with no error anywhere.
  - **Special games:** game ids `{509663, 509672}` can be earned on any channel regardless of the stream's game, in both the eligibility rule and `can_watch` (`utils.py:422, 457-458`; `inventory.py:469-476`; `twitch.py:1069-1090`). A strict "right game" twin rejects a plan that is correct live, and a strict planner skips those campaigns forever.
  - **Benefit type decides eligibility per campaign, not per drop:** `eligible` is "has any BADGE/EMOTE benefit → the badges/emotes setting, else linked" (`inventory.py:394-404`), computed over the whole campaign with `any()`. A campaign mixing a badge with a `DIRECT_ENTITLEMENT` is minable unlinked and yields only the badge; "linked, or badge/emote" alone hides that.
- **Concrete scenario:** drop1 is the precondition of drop2. End-time ordering starts with drop2. The twin, written from research 05's rules, credits drop2 anyway; 200 seeds pass and the live run mines nothing for three hours.
- **Fix:** model the drop graph (`preconditionDrops`, `requiredMinutesWatched`, `startsAt`/`endsAt`, `benefitEdges[].distributionType`, `campaign.game.id`), make the twin refuse to credit an unlockable drop, add "special game bypasses the channel-game check" as a named rule with its source, and let the planner choose the **drop** (`inventory.py:434-441`), not the campaign.

### CF-5: One per-IP token bucket inside the seam is the wrong arbitration, and under `TestClock` it is a hang

- **Where:** §3 _"A per-IP request budget (token bucket) sits inside the seam, so the twin enforces it too."_
- **Why it sinks the design:** the traffic is not one class. A watch tick is 1 media-playlist `GET` plus ~15 sequential segment `HEAD`s every 20 s (`channel.py:432-487`; `constants.py:130`) — about 48 CDN requests/min, unrelated to the GQL limit. TDM enforces the ~5 req/s limit _on GQL only_, and caps concurrency as well as rate (`twitch.py:523`; `utils.py:348-378`: `max(total, concurrent) < capacity`). Research 02's numbers are per IP _and_ per class ("~10 websockets per IP and ~5 GQL requests/s"). A single shared bucket either throttles the watch — silently breaking whatever the twin's credit rule assumes — or is set so high it enforces nothing, which is exactly where the twin is supposed to catch a bad planner. And a bucket that _waits_ inside the seam becomes unbounded virtual waiting under `TestClock`: a buggy plan hangs a seed instead of failing it, and research 03 §2's "simulated week + heap bound" test never terminates.
- **Concrete scenario:** a rescan over 60 campaigns issues `Inventory` + `Campaigns` + `ceil(60/20)` batched campaign-details requests (`twitch.py:1492-1529`) while a watch tick is in flight. With one bucket the tick's 16 requests queue behind catalog work, the 20 s interval slips to 90 s, and a tick-counting credit model under-reports. In the twin, the same plan stalls on the clock instead of failing.
- **Fix:** three classed budgets (GQL ops, HLS requests, socket connections), rate and concurrency separate, in config and in the twin; budget exhaustion is an **event** the core must handle (backoff/reschedule), never a block inside the seam; assert in the twin that no operation waits past its own deadline.

## 3. Significant Issues

1. **Five periodic fibers.** §2 lists watch tick (20 s), inventory (60 s), rescan (hourly), token (hourly) — research 04 §6.2 says "prefer one event loop per account (timer tick, socket frame, state change) over many independent periodic tasks", and open question 1 asks what 04 already answered. Derive deadlines from `State` (`nextWatchAt`, `nextInventoryAt`) and drive them from one scheduler, so `TestClock` sees a single wake-up list (04 §7's gotcha).
2. **Journal size and CPU are unbounded inside a 90 MB budget.** The recorded responses include `ViewerDropsDashboard` + `Campaigns` + batched campaign details — the largest payloads in the system, large enough that TDM ships a `dump` mode to eyeball them (`twitch.py:1541-1562`) — and a 60 s inventory poll is 1,440 of them a day. §5 says "rolling" with no byte bound, no age bound, no sampling rule, no gzip CPU budget; P3 in `open-questions.md` admits the size is unmeasured. Cap MB/day, drop bodies over N KB, record digests + shape for the rest.
3. **Redaction is one word with no gate.** AGENTS.md forbids tokens, `sig`/`token`/`hdnts` and `Authorization` in logs, fixtures and commits; research 04 §3 lists just-mining's actual leak shapes (signed URL inside an error message, headers in fields, token in a redirect fragment). There is no fixture corpus used as _inputs_, no lint, no assertion in the recorder.
4. **`doctor` has no contract and no schedule.** Research 02 §7.2 wants a scheduled read-only probe to catch hash rotation and the silent null. The proposal lists `doctor` in the CLI and nowhere else: no exit codes, no verdict, no CI use. Research 02 §2 calls the silent null "the failure mode to design for" — and TDM itself swallows it (`dropCampaigns or []`, `twitch.py:1506`) and goes Idle, which is precisely the bug the probe exists to catch.
5. **`Idle` conflates "nothing to mine" with "I cannot see the catalog".** One `Idle` state and one `Error` event means `status --json` cannot tell a homelab user the only thing they need told apart (decisions 10, 15).
6. **`pubsub` modelled as an op.** Ops are request→response; a socket is subscribe/emit/reconnect/backpressure, and re-subscription after reconnect is most of `websocket.py:137-200`. Either a callback is smuggled through the interface or the lifecycle is lost.
7. **Shape-drift faults are impossible through "the same Schemas".** §4 correctly builds twin JSON through the decoding Schemas (research 04 §2.1) and also lists a "schema-drift response" fault (04 §2). Both cannot hold unless drift is generated _after_ encoding, by mutating bytes. Say so or the fault is untestable.
8. **Milestone order inverts research 04 §3.** A full wire twin with "no live Twitch at all" (milestone 1), then the recorder (milestone 2). 04 §3 says ship the recorder with the first build and start a shadow instance as soon as discovery decodes, "so the recordings are ready by the time the twin needs calibrating". As ordered, the twin is calibrated against nothing.
9. **ACL fan-out is unpriced.** ACL channels appear only in `allow.channels` and are not in the directory query; TDM checks them online with batched GQL (`twitch.py:1660-1690`) and gives them switching precedence (`channel.py:184-188`, `twitch.py:1091-1107`). A 500-channel ACL is ~25 batched reads per rescan; the planner says "allowed" with no cap and no budget line.
10. **Identity is more than constants.** §3 puts "hashes, client ID, user agent" in one module; TDM also needs a per-session `Client-Session-Id` and an `X-Device-Id` read from the page cookie `unique_id`, and it verifies the token's `client_id` matches the selected client (`twitch.py:411-458, 459-492`). Those must be persisted per account, modelled in the twin, and the login module must pin (client ID, user agent, redirect URI) as one tuple — the implicit grant only works with the Android client ID and `https://www.twitch.tv/` as the redirect (research 02 §1).

## 4. Where the Proposal Misreads or Overstates the Research

1. **"Claim them as soon as they complete"** (Goals) is overstated: with only a 60 s inventory poll and no current-drop read, completion is discovered late by construction. TDM's `drop-claim` flow exists because inventory is too slow (`twitch.py:1239-1258`).
2. **Open question 3 assumes "no event names the drop".** `drop-progress` carries `drop_id` with `current_progress_min` (`twitch.py:1239-1297`), and `DropCurrentSessionContext` names the drop and its minutes (`constants.py:353-361`). What Twitch does not tell you is _which campaign it progresses when several share a stream_ — a narrower problem than the question states. Research 02 §3's citation of TDM #387 is stale against master.
3. **"inventory every 60 s" is presented as design, not as a choice.** Research 02 §3 attributes 60 s to just-mining and an hourly full refresh to TDM; TDM master reloads at most hourly plus on drop-claim and drop notifications (`twitch.py:1038-1067, 1280, 1305`). Adopting 60 s is a new decision with a budget and journal cost attached.
4. **The claim window is silently contradictory.** Research 02 §3 says 7 days (help page) and 24 h (its own TDM cite); research 04 §2 puts 7 days in the twin; TDM implements 24 h (`inventory.py:143-151`). The proposal picks none and inherits all three.
5. **Research 05's "TDM claims the bonus chest on the channel it watches (TDM #105)" is not in TDM master.** `ClaimCommunityPoints` and the `community-points-user-v1` topic exist and are marked "unused" (`constants.py:316-326, 503, 509`). The only implementation is TCPM — GPL-3.0, read-not-copy (02 §6b). "Points on the watched channel" is therefore a net-new unverified mutation path in v1, not a by-product to port; the twin has no source for its rules.
6. **"The PubSub socket is one fiber" contradicts the research it cites.** ~10 sockets per IP (02 TL;DR) is a budget, and TDM needs a pool of up to 8 to cover 199 channels (`constants.py:118-123`, `websocket.py:371-393`). One socket is 50 topics — 24 channels once the two base topics are spent (`twitch.py:701-707`).
7. **§2's timer list plus open question 1 contradict research 04 §6.2**, which the proposal otherwise follows: "fewer concurrent fibers means fewer interleavings to explore".
8. **"Constants (hashes, client ID, user agent) live in one module with a verified date" understates the coupling.** Swapping any element of (client ID, user agent, session id, token, redirect URI) invalidates the rest, and the WEB client additionally needs `Client-Integrity` (research 02 §1). The module's contract should be "one client identity, versioned together, each field dated when last verified live".

## 5. Over-engineered for v1 vs Missing

**Over-engineered.** `Recording(Live)` and `Replay(journal)` as two more variants of the seam, plus _strict and_ keyed replay, before a single live watch. A seven-invariant sim with shrinkers and trace digests in milestone 1. Six CLI commands (`login, run, status, doctor, sim, replay`) against decisions 15's "CLI only, bare minimum". A token bucket inside the seam. A first planner that already carries priority order, ACLs, exclusions, `DROPS_ENABLED` and viewer ordering. Cut keyed replay to v1.1 (strict replay + fixture decode tests cover the incident case), cut the planner to game priority, and add the other rules only when E1 exists.

**Missing.** `ReadCurrentDrop`. `ReadDirectory` — `gameDirectory` is an op in §3 but no Command reaches it (an internal inconsistency: the planner cannot enumerate directory channels). Current-drop and drop-claim payloads in the twin. Precondition chains and special games (CF-4). The write-ahead claim intent (CF-3). A `status --json` schema, which is the entire external surface per decisions 15. A health/exit contract a supervisor can consume — "run unattended for other people" with no signal systemd can read. Redaction tests (S3). The post-claim watch restart: after a claim the next drop needs a fresh watch, and TDM polls `dropCurrentSession` until the drop id changes (`twitch.py:1259-1281`). An explicit written decision _not_ to use `AvailableDrops` as a fallback, given 02 §2's warning about its cost (and TDM does call it per channel, `channel.py:326-334, 349-384`).

## 6. Answers to the 6 Open Questions

1. **One reducer + one scheduler fiber + one socket fiber; deadlines owned by `State`.** Not "a few fibers per concern": the five timers are the interleaving surface research 04 §6.2 tells you to delete. Ticks become `Tick(deadline)` computed from state; the scheduler compares deadlines and emits nothing else.
2. **Wire-level twin, but only where the wire _is_ the behaviour:** GQL JSON, usher/m3u8, segment `HEAD`s, socket frames. A typed-level twin is a false economy because the credit rule is literally "an HTTP verb on a playlist snippet" (`channel.py:432-487`). Constraint: encode through the same Schemas, and generate drift by mutating encoded bytes (S7).
3. **Three sources plus a bounded estimate.** `drop-progress` frames are fast truth (`twitch.py:1239-1297`); `DropCurrentSessionContext` is the re-read when progress stalls (TDM's `minute_almost_done` path, `twitch.py:972-1035`); `Inventory.currentMinutesWatched` is slow truth (`inventory.py:228-237`); on top, an optimistic estimate capped at 15 minutes that forces a _re-read_, not a switch (`inventory.py:293-296, 310-317`). Which campaign progresses when several share a stream stays a guess; bound the damage rather than trying to know it (research 02 §3, TDM #756).
4. **Required, not optional.** A 60 s inventory poll cannot substitute: per-channel stream-state frames are the only cheap "a better channel is live now" signal (CF-1), and `drop-claim` carries the `drop_instance_id` needed to claim immediately (`twitch.py:1239-1258`). Model "PubSub unavailable" as a twin degradation with a measured penalty, not a supported mode.
5. **JSON state (tmp + rename) plus gzip JSONL journal; no SQLite in v1** (research 03 §3, decisions 15). Add fsync before rename, a schema version, a write-ahead claim intent, and `journal export --since` for bug reports (04 §3). Revisit SQLite only when "what did I earn this month" exists.
6. **Ticks are deadlines derived from state, never free-running timers, and every awaited result carries the `(channel, campaign, drop, watchEpoch)` it was issued under; stale results are dropped, not applied.** Campaign ends mid-watch → claim what is claimable (CF-3), then re-plan. Game change → the stream is no longer eligible → stop watching, re-plan. Offline mid-tick → discard the tick, switch on the next plan. All three are twin actions with seeds, not prose edge cases.

## 7. Prioritized Changes Before Building

**P0**

1. Rebuild `Event`/`Command`: channel-live/stream-state/game-change events, subscribe/unsubscribe, `ReadCurrentDrop`, `ReadDirectory`, and a persisted claim intent. (CF-1, CF-2, CF-3, S5, §5)
2. Separate eligibility from actionability; pin one claim window with a citation; define claim identity `(campaignID, dropID)` and the three-way success classification. (CF-3, §4.4)
3. Add the progress model: estimate per drop with a hard cap, three truth sources, and a disagreement rule. (CF-2)
4. Put precondition chains, special games and benefit types into the model _and_ the twin, each with a cited source and date. (CF-4)
5. Replace the single token bucket with three classed budgets plus concurrency caps; make exhaustion an event, never a wait inside the seam. (CF-5)
6. Make the "no idle" invariant knowable: bound it as "≤ K × discovery interval while an eligible live channel is known eligible", with the discovery interval a twin parameter. (CF-1)

**P1** 7. One scheduler fiber; deadlines in `State`; delete the five-timer plan. (S1) 8. Bound the recorder (MB/day, body cap, sampling) and make the redaction fixture corpus + test part of `pnpm check`. (S2, S3) 9. Reorder milestones: decoders + recorder + shadow deploy before the twin. (S8) 10. Specify `status --json` (including a distinct "discovery broken" state) and `doctor`'s exit codes, then schedule `doctor`. (S4, S5) 11. Trim the CLI to `login`, `run`, `status`, `sim` for v1; add `doctor` and `replay` when lane B has data. (§5)
